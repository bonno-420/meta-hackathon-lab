## Scope note

This case's reader checks in a specific order. The research fixes the order and the language frames for that order.

## Reader's first-check item (hero requirement)

> Pay (Annual figure) + whether hours are actually bounded (flex core vs fixed). Then commute walk time.

- Hero must contain this item verbatim as a number/date. P6 defect if missing.
- For this case: annual pay is the first-check item.
- Hero skeleton: `Annual [amount] JPY — Full-time (permanent) — [district], [city]` or `Software Engineer (SaaS) — Annual [amount] — [location]`

## Audience and persona alignment

- Persona: Alex, 26, 2y backend at small agency. Wants code-review culture, regular hours, short commute. Mobile-first.
- Appeal: Mentorship and code-review first; salary as fixed fact; commute and team rhythm.
- Language implication: We/you mix with annual anchoring; mentor/training language is supportive not instructional.

## Presentation order (for generation)

Hero: Annual pay → employmentType → location. Body: 1) Role snapshot (1 sentence, no numbers) 2) Pay (Annual line) 3) Hours — Mon-Fri + flex core as one bullet 4) Location — walk time + pass 5) Team — 8-person split 6) Training — onboarding + mentor/code review. CTA: Apply with resume — casual first interview.

- Rule: one fact per bullet. No paragraph carries two facts.
- Truncation rule: first 2 bullets are decision-critical (see first-check item).
- Missing facts: omit entirely; do not write "not stated".

## Industry phrasing types (reusable frames)

1. Responsibility verbs: design / build / maintain / ship / review — imperative, no 'you will get to'.
2. Team phrasing: '4 engineers, 2 designers, 2 PMs' — list functions, not just headcount.
3. Onboarding phrasing: '3-month onboarding with dedicated mentor and weekly code review' — duration + support + cadence as one phrase, not split.
4. Commute phrasing: '5-min walk from Umeda Station; commuter pass reimbursed in full' — time + mode + reimbursement in one line.
5. Avoid: 'unlimited growth', 'work from home', 'flexible forever' — full-time SaaS postings use bounded ranges, not open-ended promises.

## Pay / term constraints

- Pay is Annual [amount]; show grouped comma verbatim.
- Caps: `pass reimbursed in full` or `pass up to [cap]/month` — never `free`.
- Annual figure appears in hero and in Pay bullet; do not rephrase between them.

## Avoid for this case (extends common §6)

- `remote` (mustNot), invented benefits, `unlimited growth`, `flexible forever`, `work from home`.

## CTA type

- Standard: Apply with resume — casual first interview. Button says Apply; reassurance underneath: casual first step, no pressure.
